01 / Scope and platform differences

This policy explains how WILDEN Memo (老野记事, the “App”) handles information on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. The developer and operator is Wei Qiang (individual developer).

It covers the offline iPhone and iPad release, iOS 1.3.5, and Mac version 2.4.5, which includes local-network peer-to-peer sync. The iOS release does not provide in-App cross-device sync. Sync on Mac does not mean that the iPhone or iPad release has the same feature. Please read the sections for the platform and version you use.

02 / How we handle information

The App processes your note titles, text, dates, card categories, attachments, and edit state on your device. We do not operate servers, cloud services, or third-party services to collect this note content, attachments, or App usage data. The App does not provide account-based cloud sync, advertising, usage analytics, or tracking, and does not automatically upload notes, attachments, crash reports, or logs to us.

The Mac version transfers information to compatible devices over local-network peer-to-peer connections, and recipients save copies. Section 4 explains how this works and the associated risks. This involves data leaving your device; it does not mean the App never connects to a network or never transfers data. The App gives us no remote access to these copies, but a receiving device’s user may read the content it stores.

If you choose to send a support or privacy inquiry by email, we receive your email address, message, and any files you include, and use them to handle your request. Please do not send private notes or sensitive information unrelated to the request.

03 / iPhone and iPad: offline notes

iOS 1.3.5 stores note cards, history, and existing attachments in local App storage and supports viewing or previewing existing attachments. It does not provide an in-App account or cross-device sync and does not use the Mac local-network sync flow to automatically send notes or attachments.

When you choose to open this policy, a support page, or an email link, your device accesses the relevant website or email service. Your notes are not automatically included in those requests. System backups and copies you make yourself are described in Sections 5 and 6.

The App does not add application-level encryption at rest to note or attachment files. Device passwords, operating-system protections, access permissions, and backup settings affect security. Local storage does not mean that others cannot access the data in any circumstance.

04 / Mac: local discovery and peer-to-peer sync

Discovery and automatic connections

Mac 2.4.5 uses Apple Bonjour and MultipeerConnectivity for nearby device discovery and peer-to-peer communication. After launch and local data loading, the App automatically advertises its sync service. Nearby compatible devices may see connection information such as the device display name, device role, and protocol version.

The App checks sync-protocol compatibility and automatically accepts invitations that meet its v4 protocol conditions. This version has no in-App sync-off switch, common-ownership verification, account pairing, pairing code, or manual approval for each connection. Protocol compatibility does not verify identity, so the App cannot ensure that connected devices are controlled or trusted by you. An unexpected compatible device may connect and receive content.

What encryption protects

Sync sessions require transport encryption provided by Apple MultipeerConnectivity. Transport encryption does not verify who owns a device, establish trust in a recipient, or mean that the App encrypts data stored on the receiving device. A device that has received data may still read or retain its local copy.

Information transferred

Sync may include Today, Common, and History cards, along with card identifiers, titles, text, dates, attachment references, modification times, deletion markers, and conflict and origin associations. Attachment descriptions include filenames, types, sizes, modification times, and SHA-256 digests; referenced attachment files may also be transferred. Devices also exchange protocol versions and timestamps used to determine updates. Digests help check file content; they do not encrypt or anonymize it.

Recipients and persistent copies

The receiving device saves merged cards, history, deletion markers, and eligible attachments locally. Its user or another person with access to that device may read or retain the copies. We cannot remotely view, recall, or delete them through the App. Avoid running the Mac App with sensitive content on untrusted networks or near untrusted devices.

05 / Storage, retention, and deletion

Notes, history, attachments, preferences, and sync status are stored on the relevant device. Retention depends on how you manage the data, device state, system backups, and copies saved on other devices. Sync exchanges data between devices; it is not a developer-operated cloud backup service.

When a Mac card is deleted, the App stores a deletion marker associated with the original card and may sync it to connected devices to handle older copies. This version does not automatically remove these markers. Deleting a card or removing an attachment reference does not delete the physical attachment file; the current flow retains those files. History may also remain.

Deleting content locally does not ensure removal of separately retained copies on receiving devices, files saved by users, or system backups. Data on different devices and in backups needs to be managed separately. Uninstalling the App, restoring a backup, or further syncing may also affect retention. In-App deletion is not a promise of secure erasure or irreversible removal of every copy.

06 / System services, third parties, and logs

The App does not integrate advertising, usage-analytics, or tracking SDKs, and has no developer account backend or automatic log-upload service. Mac note sync uses device-to-device MultipeerConnectivity sessions and does not pass through servers operated by us.

The Mac local sync-status file keeps up to the latest 40 status lines. Device or system logs may contain device display names, connection states, errors, and some attachment names. The App does not automatically upload these records to us. System-log retention and diagnostic sharing depend on the relevant system features and settings. Before voluntarily sending diagnostic files, remove personal information unrelated to the issue.

Apple operating-system and App Store services, system backups, and websites or email services you choose to use process information under their own rules. For example, visiting the policy website may allow its hosting provider to process your IP address and request logs, while email is processed by email services. These activities differ from the App automatically collecting notes. Please also consult the relevant services’ privacy policies.

07 / Your choices and rights

You can use the App’s viewing, editing, and deletion features to manage local data, and system settings to manage device access, diagnostic sharing, and backups. The Mac version automatically advertises and accepts eligible invitations after launch, with no in-App sync-off or per-device approval control. Its interface alone cannot restrict participation to your own devices. If you do not want this discovery and sync to occur, quit the Mac App and take care with your network, device access, and sensitive content.

You may use the email address below to ask about this policy or make access, correction, deletion, or other privacy requests under applicable law. Requests about information you sent us in support emails can be made to the same address. For content stored only on your device, another receiving device, or in backups, we cannot act remotely through the App; someone with access to the relevant device or backup needs to manage it.

08 / Children and shared devices

The App is a private note-taking tool, not a public social network or chat service. Manage content on shared devices carefully; Mac local-network sync may give compatible devices a copy. Parents and guardians can help minors protect personal information through device-use, access-permission, and backup settings.

09 / Changes to this policy

We will update this policy when App features, information-handling practices, recipients, or third-party services change, and identify the effective date in the published policy. Please consult the current policy for the platform and version you use.
